Quiz 2 · Question 2

Improper integrals, made finite enough to solve.

Infinity is not a number you substitute. Replace it with a temporary boundary, solve an ordinary definite integral, and then take a limit.

Your final line must say one of two things: “The integral converges to …” or “The integral diverges.”
01

What makes an integral improper?

Something about its interval or its function is not finite.

Type 1 · Infinite interval
∫a∞ f(x) dx

The interval continues forever to the right, left, or both directions.

Type 2 · Unbounded function
∫01 1/√x dx

The function becomes infinite at an endpoint or at a point inside the interval.

For your Lecture 11 material, the main focus is infinite intervals. The same big idea always applies: replace the problematic part with a limit.
02

Infinity becomes a limit

Never substitute ∞ directly into an antiderivative.

Infinite upper boundary

∫a∞ f(x) dx = limb→∞ ∫ab f(x) dx

Replace \(\infty\) with \(b\), solve from \(a\) to \(b\), then ask what happens as \(b\) grows without bound.

Infinite lower boundary

∫−∞b f(x) dx = lima→−∞ ∫ab f(x) dx

Replace \(−\infty\) with \(a\), solve from \(a\) to the fixed upper limit, then take \(a→−\infty\).

Infinity is not a number. Do not write \(F(∞)\). Write a limit such as \(\lim_{b→∞}F(b)\).
03

Convergent or divergent?

The final limit decides the answer.

Convergent

The limit exists and equals a finite number.

limb→∞ I(b) = 1

Final statement: “The integral converges to 1.”

Divergent

The limit becomes \(∞\), \(−∞\), or does not exist.

limb→∞ ln b = ∞

Final statement: “The integral diverges.”

A function approaching zero is not enough to guarantee convergence. Both \(1/x\) and \(1/x²\) approach zero, but only the second integral converges on \([1,∞)\).
04

Infinite interval on the right

Replace the upper infinity with \(b\), then let \(b→∞\).

Lecture 11 · Core example A convergent integral
∫1∞ 1/x² dx
Rewritelimb→∞ ∫1b x−2 dx
Integratelimb→∞ [−1/x]1b
Evaluatelimb→∞ (−1/b − (−1))
= limb→∞ (1 − 1/b)
As b→∞, 1/b→0.
Answer: 1. The improper integral converges.
Lecture 11 · Core example A divergent integral
∫2∞ 1/x dx
Rewritelimb→∞ ∫2b 1/x dx
Integratelimb→∞ [ln x]2b
Evaluatelimb→∞ (ln b − ln 2)
As b→∞, ln b→∞.
Answer: the improper integral diverges.
Lecture 11 · Example Exponential decay
∫0∞ e−x/2 dx
= limb→∞ [−2e−x/2]0b
= limb→∞ (−2e−b/2 + 2)
As b→∞, e−b/2→0.
Answer: 2. The improper integral converges.
05

Infinite interval on the left

Replace \(−∞\) with \(a\), then let \(a→−∞\).

Worked example Left-infinite exponential
∫−∞0 ex dx
Rewritelima→−∞ ∫a0 ex dx
Integratelima→−∞ [ex]a0
Evaluatelima→−∞ (1 − ea)
As a→−∞, ea→0.
Answer: 1. The improper integral converges.
06

Infinite in both directions

Split the integral into two separate improper integrals.

∫−∞∞ f(x) dx = ∫−∞c f(x) dx + ∫c∞ f(x) dx

The rule that matters

Usually choose \(c=0\). Evaluate the left and right integrals separately. The full integral converges only when both parts converge. If either part diverges, the full integral diverges.

Do not try to cancel \(+∞\) and \(−∞\). Each half must have its own finite limit.
07

The p-test shortcut

A fast way to predict whether power integrals converge.

IntegralConverges whenDiverges when
∫1∞ 1/xp dx p > 1 p ≤ 1
∫01 1/xp dx p < 1 p ≥ 1
Example: p = 2

∫1∞1/x² dx converges because \(2>1\).

Example: p = 1

∫1∞1/x dx diverges because \(1≤1\).

08

The Question 2 exam method

Use these five steps every time.

  1. Identify why the integral is improper and which boundary is infinite.
  2. Replace \(∞\) or \(−∞\) with a variable and write the correct limit.
  3. Integrate the now-ordinary definite integral.
  4. Evaluate the limit carefully.
  5. State the conclusion: “converges to …” or “diverges.”
Your written solution should visibly contain: limit → antiderivative → boundary substitution → limit result → conclusion.
09

Practice questions

Solve on paper first, including the limit notation. Then reveal the solution.

P1 · Right infinity
∫1∞ 1/x³ dx
limb→∞[−1/(2x²)]1b = limb→∞(−1/(2b²)+1/2). Converges to 1/2.
P2 · p = 1
∫1∞ 1/x dx
limb→∞[ln x]1b = limb→∞ln b = ∞. Diverges.
P3 · Exponential decay
∫0∞ e−x dx
limb→∞[−e−x]0b = limb→∞(1−e−b). Converges to 1.
P4 · Left infinity
∫−∞0 e2x dx
lima→−∞[e2x/2]a0 = lima→−∞(1/2−e2a/2). Converges to 1/2.
P5 · Growing function
∫0∞ x dx
limb→∞[x²/2]0b = limb→∞b²/2 = ∞. Diverges.
P6 · Coefficient
∫2∞ 4/x² dx
limb→∞[−4/x]2b = limb→∞(−4/b+2). Converges to 2.
