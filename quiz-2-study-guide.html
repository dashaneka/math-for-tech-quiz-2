Method for Question 1

  1. Identify whether the integral is indefinite or definite.
  2. Simplify the integrand if possible.
  3. Choose the correct integration rule or technique.
  4. Find the antiderivative.
  5. Add +C for an indefinite integral, or evaluate F(b) − F(a) for a definite integral.
  6. Check signs, arithmetic, and parentheses.

Checklist

The p-integral tests

Integral Converges Diverges
∫1∞ 1/xp dx p > 1 p ≤ 1
∫01 1/xp dx p < 1 p ≥ 1
Exam habit: never jump directly to an answer. Show the limit, calculate it, and finish with the word convergent or divergent.

Method for Question 2

  1. Identify why the integral is improper.
  2. Rewrite it using the correct limit.
  3. Find the antiderivative.
  4. Evaluate the limit.
  5. State convergent with its value, or divergent.
03

First-order differential equations

Recognize the equation type, separate carefully, then use the initial condition.

File to study

  • PDFLecture 12 · First-Order Differential Equations

Core forms

General first-order equation
dydx = f(x, y)
Separable equation
dydx = g(x)h(y)
Euler · next x
xn+1 = xn + h
Euler · next y
yn+1 = yn + h f(xn, yn)

Checklist

Separable-equation method

  1. Separate the variables: put all y terms with dy and all x terms with dx.
  2. Integrate both sides.
  3. Simplify and solve for y, if possible.
  4. Use the initial condition to determine C.
  5. Substitute the solution into the original equation to check it.
